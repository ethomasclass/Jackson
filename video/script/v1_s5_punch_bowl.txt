The shaking started on day one. After Jackson was sworn in in March 1829, *thousands of his fans* followed him to the White House, which was open to the public. They stood on the furniture in *muddy boots* and broke glasses and dishes. Staff finally carried the *tubs of punch* out onto the lawn so the crowd would follow the drinks outside. It may be the only time in history a crisis was solved by *relocating the punch*. Jackson reportedly slipped out and spent the night at a hotel. *At his own party.*

Once the crowd went home, Jackson got to work on government jobs, like postmasters and tax collectors. Many of those people had held their jobs for years. Jackson said *nobody should own a government job forever*, and any ordinary citizen was smart enough to do one. In practice, the jobs went to *people who had supported him*. A senator named William Marcy later defended it with the line *"To the victor belong the spoils."* {Spoils} means the prizes you take after winning. So it became known as the {spoils system}. Imagine your best friend wins class president and puts you in charge of the prom budget. Great for you. Less great for prom if you're bad at math.

Jackson also had to pick his {cabinet}, the top advisers who run departments like the Treasury and the War Department. And that's where things got *weird*.
