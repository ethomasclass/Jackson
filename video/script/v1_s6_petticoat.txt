Jackson's first big crisis wasn't a war or a money problem. It was *gossip*.

Jackson made his old friend *John Eaton* Secretary of War. Eaton had just married *Peggy Timberlake*, the daughter of a Washington innkeeper. Peggy was smart, outspoken and, according to Washington's fancy families, *not respectable*. Her first husband, a Navy officer, had died at sea, and she married Eaton only a few months later. Rumors said the two had been involved while her husband was still alive.

The wives of the other cabinet members *refused to visit Peggy* or invite her to anything. The leader of the snub was *Floride Calhoun*, whose husband, *John C. Calhoun*, was Jackson's vice president. Picture the worst cafeteria drama you've ever seen, except the lunch table is *the United States government*.

Jackson *took it personally*. Peggy reminded him of Rachel, another woman he believed gossip had destroyed. He investigated the rumors himself and called a *cabinet meeting* to announce that Peggy was *innocent*. Yes, the president of the United States held an official meeting about this.

Only one cabinet member went out of his way to be kind to the Eatons: Secretary of State *Martin Van Buren*. He was a widower, so he had no wife to object, and he was such a clever politician that people called him *"the Little Magician."* In 1831, to end the fight, Van Buren and Eaton *resigned*, which gave Jackson a reason to *replace almost his entire cabinet*. Van Buren became Jackson's closest ally and, later, his vice president. Calhoun became his *enemy*. A fight over dinner invitations had just *changed who would be the next president*, and Jackson's war with Calhoun was *only getting started*.
