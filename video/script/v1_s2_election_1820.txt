For most of the country's early years, there were two political parties: the *Federalists* and the *Democratic-Republicans*. Then, after the {War of 1812}, America's second war against Britain, the Federalists *fell apart*. They had opposed the war, and when it ended, most Americans felt like they'd won. Nobody wanted to vote for the party that had *rooted against it*. That left *one party* and one very popular president, *James Monroe*. Newspapers called this time the *Era of Good Feelings*.

Americans don't vote for president directly. Each state has {electors}, people who cast the official votes, and together they're called the {Electoral College}. In 1820, Monroe ran with *no real opponent*. Imagine running for class president and nobody else signs up. He won *every electoral vote but one*. A New Hampshire elector named *William Plumer* voted for someone else, *John Quincy Adams*. For years people said Plumer did it so George Washington would stay the only president ever elected {unanimously}. What Plumer actually said was that he thought Monroe was doing a *bad job*.

But one party can't keep everyone happy forever. When Monroe's time was up, everyone in that one party wanted his job, and the *good feelings ended fast*.
