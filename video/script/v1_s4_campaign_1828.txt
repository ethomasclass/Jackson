Here's why. In the early United States, most states let only *white men who owned property* vote. The right to vote is called {suffrage}. The idea was that owning land proved you cared about your community. But new western states needed settlers, so they let men vote *without owning land*. City workers who paid taxes and fought in wars asked why they couldn't vote too. One by one, states *dropped the property rule*. Historians call this {universal white male suffrage}. Keep the words *"white"* and *"male"* in mind. *Most Americans still couldn't vote.*

States also started letting regular voters pick their electors, instead of letting state lawmakers do it. Put it together and the numbers *exploded*. About *365,000* people voted for president in 1824. In 1828, more than *1.1 million* did.

It was a *rematch*, Jackson against Adams, and it was one of the *nastiest campaigns* in American history. Jackson's side called Adams a spoiled rich snob who had wasted public money on a "gambling device" for the White House. It was a *pool table*, and Adams paid for it himself. Adams's side printed the *"Coffin Handbill,"* a poster covered in black coffins, one for each soldier Jackson had ordered executed for deserting during the War of 1812. Then they went after his wife, *Rachel*. Years earlier, she and Jackson had married before her divorce from her first husband was final. Adams supporters called her a {bigamist}, someone married to two people at once.

Jackson won in a *landslide*. His supporters became the *Democratic Party*, the same party that still exists today. His style of politics, with rallies, parades and a candidate who says "I'm one of you," became known as {Jacksonian Democracy}. But in December 1828, weeks before he took office, *Rachel died* of a heart attack. Jackson blamed his political enemies for her death for the rest of his life. So he arrived in Washington *grieving, angry and ready to shake things up*.
