In *1806*, a man named *Charles Dickinson* insulted Andrew Jackson's *wife*. So Jackson challenged him to a {duel}, a fight with pistols where two men stand apart and take turns shooting. Dickinson was known as one of the *best shots in Tennessee*, and he *fired first*. The bullet hit Jackson in the chest, about *an inch from his heart*. Jackson pressed his hand to the wound, *stayed standing*, aimed and killed Dickinson. The bullet stayed in Jackson's chest *for the rest of his life*.

*Twenty-three years later*, that man became the *seventh president* of the United States. He was *stubborn*, *fearless*, *loyal* to his friends and *unforgiving* to his enemies. So how did a man like that end up in the *White House*? To answer that, we have to start with a time when American politics was so calm it was almost *boring*.
