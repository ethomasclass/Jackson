That new national pride needed something to stand on, and part of it was built in *factories*. Before the war, Britain guarded its factory technology like a *state secret*, especially the {power loom}, a machine that wove cloth using water power instead of human hands. It was illegal to take the machines, or even drawings of them, out of the country. In *1810*, a Boston merchant named *Francis Cabot Lowell* sailed to England for his health. He really was sick. But he also talked his way into mill after mill, walked the factory floors, and *memorized* everything: every gear, every measurement. He never wrote any of it down, so, as the story goes, when British customs searched his luggage on the way home, they found *nothing*. The design was locked in his head.

Back in Massachusetts, with the war cutting off British cloth, Lowell and a mechanic named *Paul Moody* rebuilt the power loom from memory and improved it. In *1814*, they opened a mill at *Waltham* that turned raw cotton into finished cloth under *one roof*, the first of its kind in the country. Two years later, Congress passed a {tariff}, a tax on imported goods, that made British cloth more expensive so mills like Lowell's could compete. One *stolen idea*, carried home in a man's memory, helped launch an entire *American industry*.
