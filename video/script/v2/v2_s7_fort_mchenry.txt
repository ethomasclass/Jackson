A few weeks later, the British moved on to their next target: *Baltimore*. Standing in their way was *Fort McHenry*, guarding the harbor. A lawyer named *Francis Scott Key* was on a ship in that harbor. He had just won the release of an American prisoner, and the British *wouldn't let him leave* until the attack was over. So he watched the whole battle from the water. For *twenty-five hours*, the British bombarded the fort. Key had no way of knowing who was winning. All he could do was wait for morning.

At dawn, the smoke cleared, and there it was: the American flag, *still flying* over the fort. Key was so moved that he started writing a poem on the back of a letter, about bombs bursting in air and a flag that was *still there*. That poem was later set to music. You know it today as *"The Star-Spangled Banner."* A capital in ashes, and three weeks later, the words of a future *national anthem*. That was the mood of the country by the fall of 1814.
