Meanwhile, a group of young, loud congressmen, mostly from the South and West, were getting famous for a different reason. Newspapers called them the {War Hawks}. Their leader was *Henry Clay* of Kentucky, just *thirty-four* and already Speaker of the House, the most powerful job in Congress. And the War Hawks weren't only angry about sailors. They wanted *Canada*.

Canada belonged to Britain back then. The War Hawks figured that if America was going to war with Britain anyway, why not take some of Britain's land too? One of Clay's allies, a South Carolina congressman named *John C. Calhoun*, told Congress, "I believe that in *four weeks* from the time a declaration of war is heard on our frontier, the whole of Upper Canada and a part of Lower Canada will be in our power." Four weeks. That's how confident they were.

Remember that number. It's coming back.
